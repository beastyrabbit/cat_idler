Earlier source-reviewed interruption findings were already corrected when their executable scenarios first ran. Their passing tests cover busy-worker ownership, direct-control route release, item-only pile removal, farm replacement, preserves, medicines/brew, equipment combat effects, accounting reachability, zone decisions, and bounded direct movement.

A complete final noncampaign run passed all 636 cases, including 134 regressions, in 23.2803 seconds. The 134 regressions also passed separately in 11.1153 seconds. All nine campaign twins passed in 213.7647 seconds on `61d29f1`, after the housing selectors, expansion footprint/road guards, fishing placement checks and linear-work reservations, but before the prospective farm-edge correction. Every original founding cat survived, and no colony reset. Campaigns exercise corrected housing selection after six game-hours; they contain no player road/rail construction, expansion jobs, farm corridor fences, transport routes or imported batch outputs. They were not repeated for the final edge prediction, which leaves ordinary movement unchanged. The four farm-edge scenarios directly cover that full public chain; other focused scenarios and authority/import tests cover their respective paths.

The four review regressions for carried Food, surplus Planks, Armor production and unequipping first failed together, then passed after their fixes. Existing assertions were preserved. The construction cases follow public control, pickup, reassignment, material delivery and completion, checking the exact bill and recoverable surplus. The Armor cases check accepting storage, item identity, condition and the absence of duplicate scalar goods.

Eight further cases cover new-building Food and surplus Planks, offerings, scouting, hauling, and resumed road, rail and expansion jobs. All first failed, then passed after corrections. The resumed expansion case additionally exposed the paid-perimeter cutover defect and now checks the complete outer boundary. Earlier assertions remain intact. The imported-trade cancellation guard preserves payment already earned by an outward delivery; authority and import tests provide that separate coverage rather than these route-free campaigns.

Ten further regressions cover produced Mug recovery and sale, cancellation of produced output, haul cancellation and death before and after pickup, claims and full storage, Steward recovery, source occupancy and transfer between existing stores. The public manual paths first rejected item-only sources; separate failing probes caught the Steward selector, premature source-capacity release and source selection. Final cases retain exact identity, condition, maximum condition, material and quality and reject scalar copies. The authority suite separately verifies real save/reload during pickup and blocked delivery, imported per-kind equipment capacity, and mixed batches of exact station output. It also covers public staffing of empty imported queues with one or two workers, physical output pickup, exclusive claims, full-storage blocking, restart and resumed delivery. Final delivery checks one stack or item against the receiving pile before moving it and retains pending output until its own destination has space.

Thirty territory regressions cover foreign claims and stale foreign building footprints for roads, rails, bridges, docks, wagons and vessels; changed ownership during funded work; full-square and imported single-tile expansion; fishing, zones and scaffolds; and founding and extinction recovery. The first 24 cases failed before correction, and the expanded 28-case set also failed before implementation. All 30 pass in the final run, including two later scaffold cases. Unclaimed construction remains reachable even where another village has a zone hint. Blocked work preserves identity, progress, inputs and claims, then resumes when the foreign claim is removed. The authority suite separately verifies rejection of a signed foreign-road request over a real socket.

Seventeen layout regressions cover the centered 3×3 shrine and surrounding ring, both founding blueprints, connected entrances, four exits and all eight finite starter resources at three seeds, disconnected construction, actual doorway crossing, paid road access, suspended work, legacy geometry, avoid zones and queued infrastructure collisions. The first seven cases failed before the layout correction. Four later road/rail collision cases also failed before their guard was added. Final checks preserve all held inputs and identities through blocking and resumption.

Twelve further layout interaction regressions cover automatic housing, both public housing aliases, existing exterior Fields, entrances and gather piles, loaded footprint conflicts, resumed expansion after a gather pile is removed, and placement during pending expansion. The original seven focused cases failed before implementation; the expanded eight-case version also failed against the prior simulation files in an isolated temporary project. Four later placement cases failed before their guard, including selection of a reserved doorway when an alternative was available. Housing completes through physical delivery and construction. Expansion cases use public road building, Field construction and pile designations; loaded-conflict cases explicitly inject the incompatible saved structure. The alternate-door case completes both Field and expansion, retains physical access and consumes only their bills. Temporary spills remain recoverable and do not obstruct expansion. Existing farm exclusions and legacy single-tile expansion semantics remain unchanged.

Fifteen later cases cover upstream road connectivity, fishing placement and competing linear reservations. Public road and Field construction reproduced lost access in both planning orders, and a loaded-state case reproduced expansion progressing toward the cut. Those three cases failed before the correction; a second road through an existing cardinal gate passed before and after it. Five fishing cases rejected after first failing on road, entrance, pending wall, duplicate and unmapped placement; two legal-shore controls passed throughout. Road and rail conflicts failed in both action orders before the shared wall reservation guard. The existing expansion cargo-recovery regression caught an overly broad rejection of a doorway that was already disconnected before expansion; comparing current and future roads fixed that case without changing its assertions. Direct footprint and doorway overlaps remain rejected. Future-road searches do not edit terrain, widen gates or rewrite saved layouts, and default current-road searches retain their earlier behavior.

Four farm-edge cases extend that check to fences between tiles. Three first failed: expansion admission for an existing Field, later Field placement beside pending expansion, and a loaded Field conflict. The alternate-route control passed before and after the correction. Each uses public roads, Field construction and farm designation; the successful chain finishes expansion, verifies that the road tiles survive while their shared edge becomes fenced, checks both Field entrances remain connected through the other road, and reconciles the wall bill. Loaded-state injection is explicit. Prospective searches replace only the expanding village's boundary edges; ordinary movement, other villages' edges and building doorways keep their existing rules.

New founding roads avoid building and stockpile footprints. Later buildings need an existing connected road and cannot create free paving. Authored roads are distinct from worn dirt, which does not satisfy the construction connection requirement. Legacy Unity and imported layouts retain their coordinates and footprints; the compatibility case reads `road_built` paths from the actual saved shrine footprint without rewriting terrain. Field fixtures establish their access before play, and the rail fixture uses ground outside the new shrine ring. Existing recipe and accounting assertions remain intact.

Three shipping regressions pass in the final noncampaign run. The full-source case fills and clears the port using a second cat's public deposit/pickup actions with finite goods established before play. The death case applies lethal health as its explicit fault injection, then uses public bridge construction and hauling to recover the stranded cargo. Campaign workloads create no transport routes or imported batch outputs. Shipping regressions and authority/import tests provide the direct coverage for those paths.

Campaigns compare all public world fields between hourly and minute-partitioned twins, rounded to nine decimal places for doubles, and validate item locations, claims, and beds every hour. The founding-survival assertion now retains original cat IDs so arriving cats cannot mask a founding death. The established campaign uses one staffed repeating wood-processing station with finite initial supplies; it does not establish that a complete mature economy runs indefinitely.

All 108 recipes run through physical input carrying, station work, and output delivery. All 487 research nodes are publicly purchased in a dependency traversal. Differential effects cover 75 building, 238 service, and 64 resource studies. Capacity modifiers use the authoritative capacity projection, with separate physical construction and full-storage checks; this is narrower than a complete hauling campaign for every capacity study. Exact-item output studies conserve one whole item and improve ingredient efficiency rather than emitting fractional equipment. No test calls a private simulation method or a live AI provider.

`latest-results.txt`, `campaign-results.txt` and `regression-results.txt` contain the completed executable results from the runs described above. Command and exit-status wrappers are omitted; every scenario result and final `RESULT` line is retained. Read those counts and any named failures before describing a run as successful.
